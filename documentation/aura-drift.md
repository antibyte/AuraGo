# Aura Drift

Aura Drift (`aura-drift`, category `games`) is a first-party, offline single-player racing
game for the Virtual Desktop. The default view is true 3D: Blender-built models, a three.js
r186 runtime and golden-hour diorama lighting. A Canvas 2D view remains as the fallback. The
game needs no provider, account, backend, network request or external resource at runtime.
The 3D overhaul is specified in `plans/2026-11-06-aura-drift-3d-overhaul.md`; it supersedes
the earlier top-down plan `plans/2026-11-04-aura-drift-racing-plan.md`, which is kept as history.

## Status (read first)

- **Nobody has play-tested the game or listened to the sound yet.** Driving feel, balance,
  sound mix and visual taste are unverified by a person. The tests below check contracts and
  numbers, not enjoyment. The next step is the owner playing on a real screen; tuning beyond
  the budgets and listening passes wait for that feedback.
- **Frame cost is measured on one GPU only:** an Intel Arc B580 at the High tier, 1536×1152,
  during the countdown only: about 13 ms median per frame (about 4 ms JavaScript, 9 ms GPU).
  Nothing has been measured on an integrated GPU, and nothing during a full race.
- **Audio is mostly own synthesis, 17 sound groups are CC0 recordings.** Engines, music and
  most sound groups are generated by the repository's seeded DSP scripts. The cow, sheep, crowd
  cheer, tyre squeal, metal scrape, small explosion, light, heavy and metal impacts, crate break,
  hay, cone, barrel, sea, forest, machinery and desert groups are trimmed CC0 recordings from
  OpenGameArt and BigSoundBank, pinned by SHA-256 in `assets/aura-drift/audio/sources.json` and
  credited in `ui/3d/aura-drift/audio/LICENSE.txt`. Nothing is downloaded at run time.

## Playing

Open **Aura Drift** from the Desktop start menu (Games). The garage has five tabs: mode, car,
track, setup and keys. Pick a mode, a car and a track, then start. Selecting a track applies
its recommended lap count (two laps, about three minutes); the Daily route always runs three
laps.

| Input | Action |
| --- | --- |
| Arrow keys / WASD | Steer (left and right), throttle (up), brake and reverse (down) |
| Space | Fire the equipped weapon |
| Shift | Handbrake (controlled oversteer); in the air, spin |
| E or Ctrl | Nitro (refilled by drifting). E is preferred: Ctrl+W closes a browser tab |
| Up / Down in the air | Front flip (up) and back flip (down); Left / Right in the air rolls |
| C | Cycle the camera (chase, high, drone, bumper) |
| H | Horn |
| T | Call the marshal back onto the road (not in time trial) |
| P / Escape | Pause and resume |
| R | Restart the current race |

Touch devices get eight on-screen buttons of 56 px, as the quality matrix test asserts; they
cover steering, throttle, brake, fire, handbrake and boost. Focus loss,
window deactivation and tab hiding pause the race and release every held key. Audio starts
only after a user interaction and suspends on pause. A read-only Desktop still allows racing
but does not write settings or personal bests.

Settings (saved per browser): car and livery (eight cars, four liveries each), camera,
graphics tier (auto, low, medium, high, ultra), volume, mute, reduced motion, weapons on or
off, and ghost display.

### Modes

| Mode | Opponents | Weapons | Ranking | Rules |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Quick race (`quick`) | 5 AI (6-car field) | setting | finish | After the first finisher a 45 s sweeper starts; cars still driving when it ends are out |
| Time trial (`time_trial`) | none | never | time | Ghost of your best recorded tape; rescue is refused |
| Cup (`cup`) | 5 AI | setting | points | All eight tracks in sequence; points 25-18-15-12-10-8 for positions 1 to 6; same sweeper as quick race |
| Daily route (`daily`) | 5 AI | setting | finish | Seeded from the UTC date, three laps, same sweeper as quick race |
| Drift Attack (`drift_attack`) | none | never | score | 90 s clock, only style counts; medals bronze 10 000, silver 22 000, gold 40 000 |
| Elimination (`elimination`) | 5 AI | setting | survival | Last place is out at 45 s, then every 30 s; a 5 s warning before each exit |
| Hot Potato (`hot_potato`) | 5 AI | air horn, shield, overdrive only | survival | A bomb is handed on by contact. Starts after 5 s with a 3 s gap; the fuse runs 15 s, shrinking per hand-over to a 10 s minimum; the holder is out on blast; cars within 16 m are shoved; the car that just passed it is immune for 1.5 s |

The weapon setting (Setup tab) switches pickups off in every mode that allows them. Time
trial and Drift Attack never use weapons.

## Rendering

### 3D view (default)

- Runtime: `ui/js/vendor/aura-drift/three-runtime.js`, a three.js **0.186.1 (r186)** IIFE bundle
  (908 369 bytes, manifest and licence beside it in `ui/js/vendor/aura-drift/`).
- Models: 20 GLB files, 6.6 MiB in total, under `ui/3d/aura-drift/v1`: eight cars, `items`,
  `track`, `animals`, `shared` props and eight biome scenery kits. `manifest.json` records
  per-file size, hash and car measurements; `LICENSE.txt` lists the licence. The models are made
  by `assets/aura-drift/generate.py` (Blender 5.2, deterministic output) and are untextured:
  chunky low-poly toy shapes, glossy paint through vertex colours and materials.
- Look: golden-hour diorama lighting, hemisphere and sun, a sky shader with the biome palette,
  shadow maps by tier, weather (rain, snow, fog, haze) drawn in the sky, terrain and props.
- The 3D view needs WebGL2. Without it the game falls back to the Canvas 2D view.
- On `auto`, a software GL renderer (the renderer string matches SwiftShader, llvmpipe or
  software) is rejected and the 2D view is used. An explicit graphics tier still accepts it.
- A lost WebGL context (`webglcontextlost`) switches the window to the 2D view.
- `auto` starts at High and steps down or up from the frame time (2-second windows, hysteresis,
  cooldowns).

| Tier | Pixel ratio | Shadow map | Post | MSAA | Particles | Triangle budget |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Low | 1.0 | off | off | off | 300 | 250 000 |
| Medium | 1.25 | 1024 | yes (FXAA) | off | 900 | 500 000 |
| High | 1.5 | 2048 | yes (SMAA, full bloom) | off | 2000 | 900 000 |
| Ultra | 2.0 | 2048 | yes (SMAA, full bloom, AO) | 4 | 3500 | 1 500 000 |

### Canvas 2D view (fallback)

The fallback renders the same simulation with Canvas 2D: streamed ground chunks, a true offset
road ribbon, kerbs, markings, pre-rendered car silhouettes, trackside furniture and the same
HUD. The High and Ultra tiers add a WebGL2 post pass (`aura-drift-post.js`) over the 2D frame,
which is the one place WebGL2 is used on this path.

### Steering: what was wrong and what guards it

Early builds steered the wrong way on the screen. The physics were correct: steering right
increased the heading, and the simulation, the tests and the car model agreed. The fault was the
camera. It used a fixed north-up view, and every race starts with its heading pointing down the
screen. Holding right therefore moved the car towards screen left.

The fix is the rotating chase camera (`aura-drift-camera.js`). Its 2D fallback rotates the world
so the heading points up the screen (`screenRotation`). Two guards exist:

- `scripts/test-aura-drift-camera.mjs` runs every track and the daily seed in every camera mode.
  It settles the camera, holds steer right and left, and asserts that the aim point moves right
  and left on screen in 3D and in 2D, and that the nose points up the screen.
- `TestDesktopAuraDriftSteeringBrowser` repeats the check in a real browser, in 3D and in 2D.

## Physics, tracks and style

- **Physics:** a deterministic bicycle model with front and rear slip angles, saturating tyre
  forces, load transfer, drag, rolling resistance, brake bias, handbrake rear-grip loss, damage,
  a drift-refilled nitro resource and nine surface types. It runs at a fixed 120 Hz with an
  accumulator and a five-substep catch-up clamp; rendering is independent and interpolated by the
  camera. Braking fades out at standstill, a held brake engages a bounded reverse, and steering
  is identical forward and reverse. Drafting a rival's wake trims drag and refills nitro. All
  gameplay randomness comes from a seeded PRNG; particles use a separate source.
- **Tracks:** eight authored radial-harmonic layouts with different widths, grip profiles,
  weather, lighting and checkpoint orders, plus a seeded daily generator. Closed Catmull-Rom
  splines are arc-length parameterised into roughly 4 m samples with a 64 m spatial grid.
  Ground and props are generated per 320 m chunk with an LRU cache capped by quality tier.
  `AD.validateTrack` checks length, width, spacing, corner radius and overlapping sections.
  Each layout has three timing sectors and drift zones at its sharpest corners.
- **Weather:** every race runs a seeded schedule (rain, snow, fog, mist, haze, glare, heat).
  It is part of the deterministic simulation, changes grip for every driver equally, and drives
  the score's ambience.
- **Style scoring:** sliding above 9 m/s and 10 degrees of slip starts a drift chain. Chains
  link inside a 1.5 s window, grow a multiplier up to ×8 and bank only when they end. Loose
  surfaces and drift zones pay more; near misses (a rival inside 2.7 m at closing speed) pay a
  bonus. The HUD shows pending points, the multiplier and the link timer.
- **AI and balance:** opponents use the identical solver with look-ahead pure pursuit,
  grip-aware corner speeds, an overtaking line, bounded catch-up (at most 6 % target speed, never
  grip, disclosed in all languages), nitro only on clean straights and a two-stage recovery
  (brief reverse, then a deterministic marshal respawn).
- **Replays:** the player's input is recorded as a compact integer tape. Time trial re-simulates
  that tape for the ghost; the same tape is stored with the personal best in local storage.

## Audio

The audio is shipped sample-based: engines, music and most sound groups come from the repository's
own synthesis, 17 sound groups from approved CC0 recordings (credited in
`ui/3d/aura-drift/audio/LICENSE.txt`). Every file is Ogg Vorbis under `ui/3d/aura-drift/audio/`,
described by its `manifest.json`, which marks each file `own-synthesis` or with its CC0 source id.

- **Sound effects (about 2.0 MB):** weapons, impacts, scrapes, tyre squeals, explosions, crate
  and prop breaks, crowd and animal calls, biome ambience beds and comic stingers and fanfares.
  Groups with several members are variants; one is picked at random.
- **Engines (about 0.66 MB):** eight cars times four layers (idle, low, mid, high), two seconds,
  mono and seamless. The player's engine crossfades the four layers by rpm with equal power;
  each rival plays the mid layer of its own car, spatialised.
- **Music (about 8.4 MB):** per biome three stereo stems (bed, drive, peak) of 16 bars at one
  tempo, all the same length, plus menu and results loops. The layers come in with intensity;
  the peak layer also follows nitro, the final lap and last place.
- **Fallbacks:** the sample bank loads by priority, and a missing or failed sample falls back to
  the live Web Audio synthesis (the earlier engine, music and bed code), so the game plays
  without the pack.
- **Loudness:** sound effects and engines have decoded-peak targets; each biome's music has one
  gain that puts the three stems together at -16 LUFS.

The build (`scripts/build-aura-drift-audio.mjs`) renders deterministically and `--check` verifies
the committed pack without ffmpeg. See `assets/aura-drift/audio/README.md` for the synthesis
modules, the budgets (sfx at most 4 MiB, engines at most 1.5 MiB, music at most 10 MiB) and how a
group can later be replaced by a hash-pinned CC0 recording.

**Not measured:** nobody has listened to the pack on speakers or headphones. The loudness and
peak numbers are measured; whether the mix sounds right is open.

## Accessibility

Keyboard-only start journey, visible `:focus-visible` outlines, a screen-reader control hint with
pointer-only touch controls hidden from the accessibility tree, touch targets of at least 56 px,
reduced motion (no shake, weather or speed-line motion), and no colour-only state in the HUD.
A full WCAG 2.2 AA review has not been done.

## Desktop integration

Lazy module loading, one render loop per visible window, complete disposal of listeners, RAF,
AudioContext, WebGL resources, caches and observers, `isActive` gating, `app_aura_drift` plus
`aura_drift.*` keys (349 keys) in all sixteen `ui/lang/desktop/*.json` bundles, Standard and
Fruity chrome, and app-owned icon artwork.

The loader loads 37 app modules plus the three.js runtime (`ui/js/desktop/core/module-loader.js`).
`window.AuraDriftApp.stats()` is a read-only per-window diagnostic: race state, time, speed,
pause, quality, renderer kind and caches, post status and audio status.

## Troubleshooting: empty dark window

The original release used an `ad-` class prefix (`ad-overlay`, `ad-panel`, `ad-menu`, ...). Ad
blockers such as AdGuard and EasyList hide elements matching `[class^="ad-"]` with
`display: none !important`, so the whole UI was hidden while the game itself kept running: no
JavaScript error and no failed request. All app classes use the `agdrift-` prefix, which has no
`ad` substring and cannot match those filter rules. Never reintroduce `ad-`, `ads-`, `advert-` or
`cc-` prefixes in app classes, IDs or new file names.

`TestAuraDriftAvoidsAdBlockerClassPrefix` guards the source statically, and
`TestDesktopAuraDriftAdBlockerBrowser` injects a realistic `[class^="ad-"]` blocker rule into the
browser fixture and asserts the menu stays visible. If a window is still empty, whitelist the
Desktop origin in the ad blocker and reload.

The app shell also defends against partial module sets: it verifies required factories before
boot, shows a translated error panel with the missing file names and a reload button when one is
absent, and replaces the optional WebGL2 post pass with a no-op so the game stays playable on
Canvas 2D. `TestDesktopAuraDriftStaleLoaderBrowser` covers both cases.

## Boundaries

"Believable physics" means deterministic, frame-rate independent, collision-safe, tunable and
convincing, not an engineering-grade vehicle simulation. There is no multiplayer, backend,
agent tool, config switch, external API, online leaderboard or deployment.

Still open: a human play-test on a real screen; listening passes on the sound pack; frame cost on
integrated GPUs and during full races; 30-minute soak runs; acceptance on recorded reference
hardware; engineering-grade tyre data; CC0 recordings for the groups that are still synthesis (crowd aww, penguin, llama, big
explosion, tyre stack, wind, city and snow ambience); a
track editor; branch shortcuts; and the browser steering test above.

```text
AURAGO_RUN_BROWSER_SMOKE=1 AURAGO_BROWSER_MUTE_AUDIO=1 go test ./ui -run '^TestDesktopAuraDrift' -count=1 -timeout 20m
```

What each group checks:

- **Physics** compares state hashes for identical input tapes at 30 and 144 render FPS, verifies
  solver bounds, low-grip understeer, wall contact at maximum speed, replay identity and tape
  round-tripping.
- **Tracks** validates the eight authored layouts and twenty generated daily routes: distinct
  shapes, spline round-trips, surface zones, drift zones and sector splits.
- **Balance** runs seeded AI-only laps on all eight tracks. AI laps stay within 89 to 117 s, with
  bounded wall contacts (low hundreds over a four-car race), weapon use, catch-up bounds and weather
  determinism.
- **Tricks, features, weapons, modes, world, props and banter** check the rule tables, the
  animal and crossing logic, weapon roulette and firing, mode rules (medals, sweeper, elimination,
  hot potato), world generation, prop placement and the banter lines.
- **Camera** runs every track, the daily seed and every camera mode, holds steer left and right,
  and checks the aim point on screen in 3D and in 2D (`screenRotation(pi/2)` maps car-left to
  smaller screen x).
- **Assets** checks the 20 GLBs, the manifest and budgets; `AURAGO_AURA_DRIFT_ASSETS_PARTIAL=1`
  skips the exact file-set check for partial packs.
- **Audio** checks manifest groups against the runtime contract, the engine set, aligned music
  stems, budgets and the decoded peak and loudness targets.
- **Go tests** check registration, the three-runtime load order, the locale keys in all sixteen
  languages, the ad-blocker class prefix, no external resources and the browser flows X.

### Dev server and harness

```text
node scripts/aura-drift-dev-server.mjs
```

The server listens on `127.0.0.1:8791` (or the port given as the first argument) with no-store
caching. It serves `ui/` and the harness at `/harness`, which loads the real desktop module loader
and the app outside the desktop shell:

```text
http://127.0.0.1:8791/harness?track=alpenpass&autostart=1
```

Query parameters: `track`, `car`, `mode`, `quality`, `camera`, `autostart=1`, `seed`. Use it for
real-screen play; it is not shipped (`ui/testdata` is outside the web asset set).

Ownership: `ui/js/desktop/apps/AGENTS.md` (app modules and contracts), `ui/AGENTS.md`
(asset and locale rules), `internal/desktop/AGENTS.md` (catalog entry), `assets/aura-drift/AGENTS.md`
(model kit), and the plan above.
