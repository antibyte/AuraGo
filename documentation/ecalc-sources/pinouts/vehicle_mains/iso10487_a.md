## View
Written as the standard draws it: the radio's connector (the standard calls it the "socket"; it carries the male tabs) seen from the rear of the radio, topside up. Part A is the lowest of the three parts (C top, B middle, A bottom). Rows, left to right:
- [1, 3, 5, 7]  (upper row)
- [2, 4, 6, 8]  (lower row)

The polarizing slot is on the left edge of each part in this view, and contact 1 is marked with a dot at the upper left. The other view (mirror) is the vehicle harness plug (receptacles) seen at its mating face: [7, 5, 3, 1] / [8, 6, 4, 2], slot on the right.

Basis: ISO 10487-1:1992, figure 1, "Rear view" with "Topside" marked: detail D shows Part C (contacts 1 to 10), Part B and Part A (contacts 1 to 8) with "1 3 5 7" on the upper row and "2 4 6 8" on the lower row of each part, a dot at contact 1, and the step of the polarizing slot at the left; legend "2) Axes of polarizing slots". Text 3.1: "The sockets of the multi-pole connector and the antenna sockets shall be mounted at the back of the radio"; 3.2: "The plug forms a part of the vehicle harness"; 3.4: up to 26 male contacts in parts A, B and C, tabs 2,8 × 0,5 to ISO 8092-3; 3.5: each part of the plug has a polarizing key.

## Cross-check
The standard is the authority; the preview contains its complete normative content (pages 1 to 3), so no second source is needed for the allocation. Points to watch:
- A4 and A7: the standard does not say "A4 = permanent, A7 = ignition". It says A7 is the main supply (permanent, or switched only if A4 is not switched) and A4 is a switched positive of at most 0.3 A (only if A7 is not switched). Many vehicles and aftermarket radios use their own split (search results, not counted, report A4 permanent / A7 switched on some makes and the reverse on some radios); the notes must tell users to measure.
- A5 is an output of the radio (antenna supply, 0.3 A max), not a supply into the radio. Using it for a power amplifier's remote input is common practice but not in the standard.
- Contacts 9 and 10 of part A do not exist (table 1 footnote 3).

## Choices
- Catalog item: `rows` 2x4 (`1 3 5 7` / `2 4 6 8`), `outline: rect`, `contact: blade`, `drawn: plug` (the radio's tabs from the rear), `key: left` (the polarizing slot on the left edge). Views as the "App labels" bullet below. The item notes carry the one-sentence safety line, the A4/A7 difference to the common "A4 permanent / A7 ignition" folklore (measure both), the A5 remark and the missing contacts 9 and 10. No `wire`.
- Names are short forms of table 1 (1 to 24 characters).
- A1 `signal`; A3 `nc` ("For future application to be determined"); A4, A5, A6, A7 `power`; A2 and A8 `ground`.
- `type: rows`, `outline: rect`, `contact: blade` (tabs 2,8 × 0,5 mm), drawn view as above. App labels: plug view = "Radio's connector, rear view (tabs; ISO: socket)", socket view = "Harness plug (receptacles)". Key/slot on the left side in the drawn view.
- No `wire`: the standard gives no colours.
- Recommended notes: "Measure A4 and A7 in the car before connecting a radio: the standard lets either be the switched one. A5 powers an antenna amplifier from the radio (max. 0.3 A)."

## Safety note
Vehicle 12 V wiring can deliver very high short-circuit currents: a shorted battery positive can melt wires and start a fire, and A7 can be live with the ignition off. Fuse every added positive line and disconnect the battery before working behind the dashboard; vehicles with airbags and data buses may need a workshop. Currents here are only those stated in the standard (0.3 A max for A4 and A5).
