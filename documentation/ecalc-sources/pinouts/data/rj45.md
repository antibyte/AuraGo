# rj45 — RJ45 (8P8C) / RJ45 (8P8C)

Kind: cross-checked reproductions
Pins: 8; types: data 8 (each variant)

## View
Written for the jack, looking into its opening, with the latch notch at the bottom and the contacts at the top (`drawn: socket`): 1 2 3 4 5 6 7 8 left to right.

The other view is the plug seen from its tip, latch at the bottom: 8 … 1 left to right. With the plug turned so that the latch is on top, it reads 1 … 8, and `notes` says both, because this is where people go wrong.

Basis, seen in figures (no text states a view):
- Barco Event Master user guide, image A-3 "Ethernet connector" (a device port): notch at the bottom, 1 on the left, 8 on the right.
- pinouts.ru figure rj45f.gif: same.
- L-com drawing TDG1026-8R, jack J1: drawn with the notch at the top, callouts "PIN 8" on the left and "PIN 1" on the right. A half turn gives the same picture.
- pinouts.ru figure rj45m.gif, plug "FRONT": latch at the top, contacts at the bottom, 1 on the left, 8 on the right. This is the half-turned mirror image of the jack, as the two faces require.

- Winford Engineering, PBC8P8C, "8P8C / RJ45 Jack Pinout": notch at the bottom in the rendered face view; the numbers are not drawn by my renderer, but the text layer has 1 before 8, left to right.

**For the drawing:** the view holds only if the `rj45` outline has its notch at the bottom. The catalog schema has no `key` for this outline.

## Cross-check
Colours: NetAlly and Seetronic agree on every pin of both schemes. Microchip's pair colours and Barco's T568A-pattern table agree. TIA-568 itself is paywalled and was not read.

Signals: Barco names 1000BASE-T as TX0+, TX0-, RX0+, TX1+, TX1-, RX0-, RX1+, RX1-; NetAlly as TxRx A+, A-, B+, C+, C-, B-, D+, D-. Same pins and pairing, the letters of NetAlly are used. On 10/100BASE-T, Barco (a device) has TXD on 1, 2 and RXD on 3, 6; NetAlly (a switch) has Rx on 1, 2 and Tx on 3, 6. They agree once the side is taken into account; `notes` says so.

PoE pairs: NetAlly, GlobTek, L-com, Microchip and the IEEE contribution all agree: 1,2,3,6 and 4,5,7,8.

Disagreement, polarity: NetAlly and GlobTek give pins 1, 2 DC+ and pins 3, 6 DC-. Microchip's table for Type 4 gives pins 1, 2 negative and 3, 6 positive, with several options for Type 3. L-com says polarity is not specified in 802.3af, and Wikipedia says the data pairs change polarity between MDI and MDI-X. So I state no polarity, only which pins form one pole, and warn in `notes`. Pins 4, 5 positive and 7, 8 negative is what NetAlly, GlobTek and Microchip's Type 4 column share, but Type 3 allows the swap, so that stays out too.

Words: the IEEE contribution and Microchip use "Alternative" at the power source and "Mode" at the powered device; NetAlly and GlobTek use "Mode" for both. `notes` uses Mode, as asked for and most searched.

## Choices
- Names combine the 10/100 and 1000BASE-T terms, "TX+ / A+" and so on, within 24 characters. The catalog schema's sample says "TX+".
- All pins are `data`; PoE does not change the type.
- The same pin notes in both variants. Wire colours are the only difference, as both colour sources show.
- No crossover cable table: not part of this item.
