# rj11 — RJ11 (6P4C) / RJ12 (6P6C) telephone jack / RJ11 (6P4C) / RJ12 (6P6C) Telefonbuchse

Kind: free official specification (US 47 CFR Part 68, Subpart F, 10-1-00 edition — the FCC rule that defined the USOC jack wirings until 2001) for the line assignment; the maker's own drawing (Würth Elektronik) for the contact numbering, cross-checked with the CFR jack figure and a device maker (Aastra)
Connector: miniature 6-position modular jack/plug (6P). RJ11 jacks commonly carry 4 contacts (positions 2–5, "6P4C") or 2 (positions 3–4), RJ12 jacks all 6 ("6P6C"). Drawn side: the jack (`drawn: socket`).
Pins: 6 positions per variant. Types: `rj11` (6P4C): signal 4, nc 2 (positions 1 and 6 have no contact). `rj12` (6P6C): signal 4, nc 2.

## Line assignment (47 CFR 68.502, wiring diagrams)
- **RJ11C / RJ11W** (single line, §68.502(a)(1), "Bridged T/R; 6-position jack"): jack conductor 3 = R (ring), conductor 4 = T (tip). "Conductors 1, 2, 5 and 6 are reserved for telephone company use." Wiring diagram: plug positions "3" "4" labelled "R" "T" to the registered terminal equipment.
- **RJ14C / RJ14W** (two lines, §68.502(c)(1), "Bridged T/R; 6-position jack"): plug positions labelled, left to right over positions 2–5: **T2, R1, T1, R2** — line 1 on 3 (R1) and 4 (T1), line 2 on 2 (T2) and 5 (R2).
- Positions 1 and 6: no assignment in RJ11C/RJ14C; reserved for telephone company use (RJ11C text).

## Pins
The `rj11` and `rj12` variants share the numbering. The names follow RJ14C, because a 4-contact RJ11 cable/jack carries both pairs; on a single-line RJ11C installation only 3 and 4 are used.

Naming note: German functions use "Tip"/"Ring"; a mapping to the German a/b wires was not verified and is left out. Names must stay ≤ 24 characters; "T1", "R1" etc. are the CFR's own labels.

No wire colours: neither the CFR diagrams nor Würth name any.

## View
Written for the **jack, looking into its opening, latch notch at the bottom, contacts at the top** (`drawn: socket`): **1 2 3 4 5 6 left to right** — the same orientation as the `rj45` outline (notch at the bottom, pin 1 on the left).

The plug seen from its tip with the latch at the bottom reads 6 … 1 left to right; with the latch on top it reads 1 … 6. (Mirror rule, as in `rj45`.)

Basis:
- Würth Elektronik, WR-MJ Modular Jack Tab Up 615006138421 (6PxC), page 1 "Dimensions": front view of the jack opening with the latch notch ("tab up") at the top and the contact fingers at the bottom, labels "6" on the left and "1" on the right. Turned by 180° (a rotation): notch at the bottom, 1 on the left, 6 on the right. The hole pattern on the same page labels the PCB holes "1" and "6".
- 47 CFR 68.500(b), Figure 68.500(b)(2)(i) "6 Position Jack Mechanical Specification": rendered and turned upright: the jack face with the contact fingers at the top, the latch opening at the bottom and a "6" next to the right-most contact. Consistent with Würth. (Only the "6" is legible; I rely on Würth for the full order.)
- The CFR wiring diagrams number the positions 1 … 6 left to right in a schematic row; they show no physical view.

**For the drawing:** a new `rj11` outline (narrower than `rj45`) with the latch notch at the bottom, 6 contact positions; the 6P4C variant leaves positions 1 and 6 empty.

## Cross-check
Line 1: CFR RJ11C (3 = R, 4 = T) and Aastra (3 Ring, 4 Tip) agree. Line 2 (2 = T2, 5 = R2) rests on the CFR RJ14C diagram alone, which is the official rule, so it is enough.
Numbering direction: Würth (maker) and the CFR jack figure agree (6 on the right with the contacts on top); the same convention as the repo's `rj45` record (notch at the bottom, 1 on the left).

## Choices
- Catalog item: `data_av.yaml`, group `data`, outline `rj11`, `contact: blade`, `drawn: socket`, one row 1..6 (as the fixture); variants `rj11` (6P4C) and `rj12` (6P6C) with identical numbers, positions 1 and 6 as `nc` pins named `NC` in both (no contact in 6P4C, reserved in 6P6C) so the page draws them as empty/NC; line 1 on 3 (R1) and 4 (T1), line 2 on 2 (T2) and 5 (R2) after 47 CFR 68.502; the notes say that RJ12 is a trade name and that TIA-968 was not read.
- One item, two variants `rj11` (6P4C) and `rj12` (6P6C) with identical position numbers 1–6. 6P4C positions 1 and 6 are `nc` (no contact) — say so in the variant notes so the drawing can grey them out.
- "RJ12" is not a USOC code in the CFR text read (RJ11, RJ14, RJ15, RJ18, RJ21, RJ26, RJ27, RJ31, RJ38, RJ41, RJ45 appear); it is used here only as the common trade name for a 6P6C jack. Say so in `notes`.
- Tip/ring typed `signal` (analogue line). No polarity or voltage given.
- Some makers number a 6P4C part's four contacts 1–4 instead of 2–5 — **not verified**, so not stated as fact; `notes` can say "positions are counted 1–6 across the jack, as the CFR does".
