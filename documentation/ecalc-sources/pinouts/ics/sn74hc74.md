# sn74hc74 — SN74HC74 dual D flip-flop with preset and clear (DIP-14) / SN74HC74 zweifaches D-Flipflop mit Setz- und Löscheingang (DIP-14)

Kind: authoritative maker documents (primary Texas Instruments, cross-check onsemi)
Pins: 14; types: power 1, ground 1, signal 4, data 8

## Part
SN74HC74N: two independent positive-edge-triggered D flip-flops, each with asynchronous preset and clear, in the 14-pin plastic DIP (TI package N, "PDIP (14)"). Suggested display name `SN74HC74 (DIP-14)`, the same in all locales.

Members that share this pinout, as far as a datasheet says so:
- SN74HC74 in SOIC (D), SSOP (DB), SO (NS) and TSSOP (PW), and SN54HC74 in CDIP (J) and CFP (W): TI's pin figure and the D/DB/N/NS/PW/J/W column of the Pin Functions table are captioned for all of them.
- onsemi MC74HC74A and MC74HCT74A: one document and one pinout figure for both; page 1 says "The MC74HC74A is identical in pinout to the LS74".
- Not this pinout: the 20-pin LCCC (FK) of the SN54HC74.

Suggested item note (TI pin figure and Table 8-1): flip-flop 2 repeats the order of flip-flop 1 from pin 13 down to pin 8 (CLR, D, CLK, PRE, Q, ~Q), so it sits one row lower: ~2CLR on pin 13 faces 1D on pin 2. ~PRE and ~CLR act at once, without the clock, and are active low; hold both high for clocked operation. If both are low together, Q and ~Q are both high, and that state does not persist when they return high. / Flipflop 2 wiederholt die Reihenfolge von Flipflop 1 von Pin 13 abwärts bis Pin 8 (CLR, D, CLK, PRE, Q, ~Q) und liegt damit eine Reihe tiefer: ~2CLR an Pin 13 liegt gegenüber von 1D an Pin 2. ~PRE und ~CLR wirken sofort, ohne Takt, und sind aktiv low; für den getakteten Betrieb beide auf high halten. Sind beide gleichzeitig low, sind Q und ~Q beide high; dieser Zustand bleibt nicht erhalten, wenn sie wieder high werden.

## View
Shape `dip`, 14 pins: package from above, pin 1 top left, numbers counter-clockwise. Basis: the pin figure in TI section 5 (no figure number in this revision) is captioned "D, DB, N, NS, PW, J, or W Package", "14-Pin SOIC, SSOP, PDIP, SO, TSSOP, CDIP, or CFP" and "Top View". It shows 1–7 down the left (1CLR, 1D, 1CLK, 1PRE, 1Q, 1Q-bar, GND) and 14–8 down the right (VCC, 2CLR, 2D, 2CLK, 2PRE, 2Q, 2Q-bar), with a small circle inside the body next to pin 1 as the pin 1 mark; no notch. I rendered PDF page 3 to an image, enlarged the figure three times and read each label and overbar. onsemi's Figure 2 "Pinout (Top View)" draws the package upright with a dot at pin 1 and the same pin order.

## Cross-check
Both sources agree pin by pin on all 14 pins: clear/reset 1 and 13, data 2 and 12, clock 3 and 11, preset/set 4 and 10, Q 5 and 9, inverted Q 6 and 8, GND 7, VCC 14. Inside TI's document the pin figure and the Pin Functions table agree; inside onsemi's document Figure 1 (with pin numbers, "PIN 14 = VCC", "PIN 7 = GND") and Figure 2 agree. The function tables agree: preset low gives Q high, clear low gives Q low, both low give Q and Q-bar high (TI: "nonstable"; onsemi: "unpredictable if Set and Reset go high simultaneously"), and with both high a rising clock edge copies D to Q.

Differences, none of them in the pin numbers:
- Names: TI `1CLR`, `1PRE`, `1CLK`, `1D` with overbars on CLR and PRE; onsemi `RESET 1`, `SET 1`, `CLOCK 1`, `DATA 1`. onsemi's pinout prints no overbar on RESET and SET; its logic diagram draws inversion circles on them, and its function table gives the same polarity as TI.
- Outputs: TI `1Q` and `1Q` with overbar; onsemi `Q1` and `Q1` with overbar.
- Packages: onsemi lists SOIC-14 NB and TSSOP-14, no DIP.
- Absolute maximum supply: TI −0.5 V to 7 V; onsemi −0.5 V to +6.5 V. Recommended range 2 V to 6 V in both (onsemi gives 4.5 V to 5.5 V for the HCT version).

## Choices
- Names follow TI, the primary source.
- Overbars: TI prints an overbar on CLR, PRE and the inverted Q. In the Pin Functions table it stands over the letters only (`1 CLR`, `1 Q`); in the figure the bar of pin 1 also covers the digit, the others do not. This record writes the whole name with a leading `~`: `~1CLR`, `~1PRE`, `~1Q`, `~2Q`, `~2PRE`, `~2CLR`. The `~` must stay on `~1Q` and `~2Q` in the catalog even if the other overbars are dropped as in the shipped `sn74hc138` item: without it pins 5 and 6 would both be named `1Q`, and pins 8 and 9 both `2Q`. The shipped `sn74hc165` item keeps `~QH` for the same reason.
- Types: D, CLK, Q and inverted Q are `data`, PRE and CLR `signal`. This follows the shipped register items (`sn74hc595`, `sn74hc165`): data lines and the clock that stores them are `data`, asynchronous controls are `signal`. A flip-flop is a one-bit register and TI calls D the "Data Input". Typing all twelve logic pins `signal`, like the gates, would also be defensible.
- Pin 14 text uses TI's recommended range 2 V to 6 V (section 6.3).
- TI calls the flip-flops "Channel 1" and "Channel 2"; the function text says "Flip-flop".
- The 20-pin FK package is left out.
- Catalog names: `~1CLR`, `~1PRE`, `~2PRE`, `~2CLR` become `1CLR`, `1PRE`, `2PRE`, `2CLR` with "active low" in the function text, as in `sn74hc138`; `~1Q` and `~2Q` keep their `~`, as `~QH` does in `sn74hc165`.
