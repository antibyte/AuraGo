# cd4017 — CD4017B decade counter with 10 decoded outputs (DIP-16) / CD4017B Dekadenzähler mit 10 dekodierten Ausgängen (DIP-16)

Kind: authoritative maker documents (primary Texas Instruments, cross-check onsemi and Renesas)
Pins: 16; types: power 1, ground 1, signal 14

## Part
CD4017BE: CMOS five-stage Johnson decade counter with ten decoded outputs and a carry output, in the 16-pin plastic DIP (TI: "16-lead dual-in-line plastic package (E suffix)"). Suggested display name `CD4017B (DIP-16)`, the same in all locales.

Members that share this pinout, as far as a datasheet says so:
- CD4017B in the ceramic DIP (F3A), SOP (NSR), SOIC (M, M96) and TSSOP (PW, PWR): TI's sheet shows one terminal diagram for the CD4017B and lists these packages.
- onsemi MC14017B: its page 1 says "Pin-for-Pin Replacement for CD4017B", and its pin assignment has the same numbers.
- Renesas CD4017BMS (the high-reliability version in ceramic packages): same numbers in its "Pinouts" figure.
- Not this pinout: the CD4022B, the octal counter on the same TI sheet. It has its own terminal diagram with two NC pins and other output positions.

## View
Shape `dip`, 16 pins: package from above, notch at the top, pin 1 top left, numbers counter-clockwise. Basis: TI's page 1 has the drawing captioned "TOP VIEW", "CD4017B TERMINAL DIAGRAM". It shows the notch on top, a dot at pin 1, 1–8 down the left and 16–9 down the right. TI's sheet is a scan of the Harris data sheet, so the labels are small bitmap digits: I rendered PDF page 1 to an image, enlarged the terminal diagram and the functional diagram six times, and read each digit. The same pin numbers stand a third time in the logic diagram on PDF page 2, which I enlarged as well. Renesas prints the same drawing ("CD4017BMS TOP VIEW") as sharp vector text, and it shows the same digit at every pin.

## Cross-check
All three sources agree pin by pin on all 16 pins in their pin drawings: outputs 5, 1, 0, 2, 6, 7, 3 on pins 1 to 7, VSS on 8, outputs 8, 4, 9 on pins 9 to 11, carry out on 12, clock inhibit on 13, clock on 14, reset on 15, VDD on 16. Inside TI's document the terminal diagram, the functional diagram and the logic diagram agree on every pin. The behaviour agrees too: counting on the rising clock edge while pin 13 is low, reset high gives output 0, and the carry is high for counts 0 to 4 (TI timing diagram; onsemi: "If n < 5 Carry = 1, otherwise = 0").

Disagreement inside onsemi's document: the "LOGIC DIAGRAM" on page 2 of MC14017B/D Rev. 10 labels pin 9 as "Q3". The same document's pin assignment (page 1) and block diagram (page 2) give pin 9 as Q8 and pin 7 as Q3, and the logic diagram itself also has Q3 at pin 7 and no Q8 anywhere. I chose pin 9 = output 8: TI shows it in three figures, Renesas in two, and onsemi in two of its three. The "Q3" at pin 9 is a labelling error in that one onsemi drawing. I report it in case a reader compares.

Other differences, none of them in the pin numbers:
- Output names: TI and Renesas print the bare digits "0" to "9"; onsemi prints `Q0` to `Q9`.
- Pin 13: TI and Renesas `CLOCK INHIBIT` (high stops the counter); onsemi `CE` with an overbar, "Clock Enable" (low lets it count). Same pin, same polarity, opposite naming.
- Pin 12: TI and Renesas `CARRY OUT`; onsemi `Cout`.
- Packages: onsemi's document lists SOIC-16 only, no DIP. Renesas lists ceramic DIPs and a ceramic flatpack. TI's sheet is the only one of the three that names the plastic DIP.
- Supply: recommended 3 V to 18 V in TI and onsemi. Absolute maximum: TI −0.5 V to +20 V (Renesas' page 1 likewise says "20V Rating"; its ratings table was not read); onsemi rates the MC14017B at −0.5 V to +18.0 V.
- Renesas' functional diagram says "VCC = 16" where its pinout says VDD. Same pin.

## Choices
- Catalog item: Catalog names of the outputs are onsemi's `Q0` to `Q9` instead of TI's bare digits; the function text names the count. Pin 9 is output 8: onsemi's logic diagram labels it "Q3", a labelling error in that one drawing (see Cross-check).
- Output names: the Name column holds TI's label, the bare digit, because the primary source rules. Recommendation for the catalog: use onsemi's `Q0` to `Q9` instead. A bare digit beside a pin number is easy to misread on a wiring page (pin 3 is "0", pin 1 is "5"), and `Q0` to `Q9` is a name read in a maker's datasheet for the pin-for-pin replacement, not an invented one. This is the one naming decision in this batch that I leave open; if `Q0` to `Q9` is taken, the spot-check names below change the same way and the function text keeps the digit.
- No pin of the CD4017B carries an overbar at TI. Pin 13 stays `CLOCK INHIBIT` (active-high inhibit) and is not renamed to onsemi's active-low `CE`. For the record: the existing `sn74hc595` item drops overbars and writes bare names (`OE`, `SRCLR`) with "active low" in the function text; the other records of this batch mark an overbar with a leading `~`.
- All logic pins are `signal`, the clock included: this is a counter, not a serial data interface.
- Pin 16 text uses the recommended range 3 V to 18 V from TI's operating conditions, which is also onsemi's range, not TI's 20 V absolute maximum.
- "Output 0 goes high" after reset is read from TI's timing diagram and onsemi's truth table.
- TI's footnote on page 1 says pin 13 can serve as the clock instead, with pin 14 tied high, and then counts on the falling edge. That is left out of the pin text to keep it short; it could go into the item note.
- "do not leave these inputs open" in the suggested note rests on onsemi's page 1 ("Unused inputs must always be tied to an appropriate logic voltage level"), not on TI's sheet.
- The CD4022B on the same sheet is left out.
