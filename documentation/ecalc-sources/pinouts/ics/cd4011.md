# cd4011 — CD4011B quad 2-input NAND gate (DIP-14) / CD4011B vierfach NAND-Gatter mit 2 Eingängen (DIP-14)

Kind: authoritative maker documents (primary Texas Instruments, cross-check onsemi and Renesas)
Pins: 14; types: power 1, ground 1, signal 12

## Part
CD4011BE: CMOS quad 2-input NAND gate in the 14-pin plastic DIP (TI: "14-lead dual-in-line plastic packages (E suffix)"). Suggested display name `CD4011B (DIP-14)`, the same in all locales.

Members that share this pinout, as far as a datasheet says so:
- CD4011B in the ceramic DIP (F3A), the small-outline packages (M, MT, M96, NSR) and TSSOP (PW, PWR): TI's sheet shows one terminal assignment for the CD4011B and lists these packages.
- onsemi MC14011B: its page 1 says "Pin-for-Pin Replacements for Corresponding CD4000 Series B Suffix Devices", and its pin assignment has the same functions at every pin.
- Renesas (formerly Intersil/Harris) CD4011BMS, the high-reliability version in ceramic packages: same functions at every pin (see Cross-check for a label swap at pins 8 and 9).
- Not this pinout: the CD4012B (dual 4-input) and CD4023B (triple 3-input) on the same TI sheet, and the 74HC00 (see note).

## View
Shape `dip`, 14 pins: package from above, notch at the top, pin 1 top left, numbers counter-clockwise. Basis: TI's page 1 (sheet 3-26) has the drawing captioned "TERMINAL ASSIGNMENTS", "TOP VIEW", "CD4011B". It shows the notch on top, a dot at pin 1, 1–7 down the left (A, B, J = AB-bar, K = CD-bar, C, D, VSS) and 14–8 down the right (VDD, H, G, M = GH-bar, L = EF-bar, F, E). TI's sheet is a scan of the Harris data sheet: I rendered page 1 at five times the base resolution and read the terminal assignment and the "CD4011B FUNCTIONAL DIAGRAM" label by label. onsemi's "PIN ASSIGNMENTS" (page 2) draws the package upright with a dot at pin 1; Renesas draws it with a notch at the top ("CD4011BMS TOP VIEW").

## Cross-check
All three sources agree on the function of every pin: inputs on 1, 2 (gate to pin 3), 5, 6 (gate to pin 4), 8, 9 (gate to pin 10) and 12, 13 (gate to pin 11); VSS 7; VDD 14. Inside TI's sheet the terminal assignment and the functional diagram agree on every label. Inside onsemi's sheet the logic diagram (1, 2 → 3; 5, 6 → 4; 8, 9 → 10; 12, 13 → 11; "VDD = PIN 14", "VSS = PIN 7") and the pin assignment agree.

Label disagreement, no functional difference: TI labels pin 8 `E` and pin 9 `F`; Renesas labels pin 8 `F` and pin 9 `E`, in both its pinout and its functional diagram. Both pins are the two inputs of the gate whose output is L = NOT(E·F) on pin 10, and a NAND gate does not care which input is which, so the wiring is the same either way. The table follows TI. onsemi names them `IN 1C` (pin 8) and `IN 2C` (pin 9).

Other differences, none of them in the pin numbers:
- Names: TI and Renesas use letters (`A` to `H` for inputs, `J`, `K`, `L`, `M` for outputs, each with its NAND expression); onsemi uses `IN 1A`, `IN 2A`, `OUT A` and so on, with the gate letter as a subscript.
- Packages: onsemi's document lists SOIC-14 and TSSOP-14, no DIP; Renesas lists braze-seal and frit-seal ceramic DIPs and a ceramic flatpack. TI's sheet is the only one of the three that names the plastic DIP.
- Supply: recommended 3 V to 18 V in TI and onsemi. Absolute maximum: TI −0.5 V to +20 V ("20-Volt Rating"); onsemi −0.5 V to +18.0 V.

## Choices
- Names follow TI, the primary source: the single letters of the Harris drawing. Alternative for the catalog: onsemi's `IN 1A` … `OUT D` (written inline), which say at a glance which pins are outputs; I leave TI's letters because the function text already says "output", the letters match the NAND expressions printed beside the pins, and no letter can be mistaken for a pin number.
- The output names are written `J`, `K`, `L`, `M` without the expression ("J = AB" with an overbar in TI); the expression goes into the function text.
- Gates are numbered 1 to 4 in the order of onsemi's gates A to D; TI and Renesas give the gates no number.
- All gate inputs and outputs are `signal`.
- Pin 14 text uses the recommended range 3 V to 18 V of TI and onsemi, not TI's 20 V absolute maximum.
- "Tie unused inputs to VSS or VDD" rests on onsemi page 1 ("Unused inputs must always be tied to an appropriate logic voltage level (e.g., either VSS or VDD)"), not on TI's sheet.
- The 74HC00 comparison in the note is read from this table and the sn74hc00 record.
