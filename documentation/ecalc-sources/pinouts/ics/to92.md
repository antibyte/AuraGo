# to92 — TO-92 examples / TO-92: Beispiele

Kind: authoritative maker document (one per part)
Pins: 3 per variant, 7 variants; types: bc547, q2n2222, q2n3904, q2n7000: signal 3 each; lm35: power 1, signal 1, ground 1; reg78l05: power 2, ground 1; tl431: signal 2, ground 1

## View
Every variant is written for the same view, the shape `to`, package `to92`: flat side towards the viewer, leads pointing down, positions `1`, `2`, `3` from left to right.

How a datasheet's drawing maps to that view, which I rendered to images and looked at:

- **View from the lead end, flat side at the top of the drawing:** rotating the part about the left-right axis until the leads point down brings the flat side to the viewer and keeps left and right. Left to right stays as drawn.
- **View from the lead end, flat side at the bottom of the drawing:** left and right swap.
- **"Top view" lying on its side, flat side on the right, leads going away from the viewer:** the top item ends up on the right in the front view, the bottom item on the left.

## Position numbers against datasheet numbers
The numbers in this item are always positions in the front view, as the task asks. They equal the maker's own numbers except in one case:

- **TL431, TI:** Table 5-1 gives LP as CATHODE 1, ANODE 2, REF 3. So TI's pin 1 (CATHODE) is at position 3 and TI's pin 3 (REF) is at position 1. This is stated in the pin notes of the variant. onsemi's TL431/D numbers the same leads 1 Reference, 2 Anode, 3 Cathode, equal to the positions.

TI's own material is not consistent here: the LP0003A outline puts pin 1 on the left in the front view, while the TL431 table and top-view figure put number 1 (CATHODE) on the right. I follow the figure with its names, since it states where each named lead is, and because onsemi's independent document gives the same physical order (reference on the left, cathode on the right).

## Cross-check
- One maker document is enough per part; 78L05 and TL431 also have a second maker, and both agree on the physical order.
- bc547: onsemi prints "OBSOLETE" across page 1 of the BC546/D sheet, a notice about onsemi's own production. The pinout is still what that sheet states; it was not checked against other makers.
- q2n7000: the same onsemi sheet numbers the SOT-23 2N7002 differently (1 Gate, 2 Source, 3 Drain). Only the TO-92 row is used.
- Case outlines: BC546/D and 2N3903/D use case 29-11, NDS7002A/D uses 135AN, MC78L00A/D uses 29-10, PN2222A uses Fairchild's ZA03D. All have pin 1 on the left in the front view.
- A web search summary reported a "conflict" for the 2N7000; reading the sheet shows it was the SOT-23 row. No real conflict.
- Nothing UNVERIFIED and no GEOMETRY UNVERIFIED.

## Choices
- **q2n2222 is the PN2222A.** The variant's name says `PN2222A`, the id stays `q2n2222`, and `2n2222` is a tag so that a search finds it. The item's notes and the note on pin 1 say it does not apply to the metal-can 2N2222, whose TO-18 can has its leads in a triangle, or to the P2N2222A, which runs the other way round (collector left). I chose the PN2222A because a maker's datasheet for it exists; a true "2N2222A in TO-92" is not in the sources I read.
- Transistor leads are `signal`: none of them is a supply by nature. The short names `C`, `B`, `E`, `S`, `G`, `D` stay language-neutral, and the words are in the function.
- `ANODE` of the TL431 is `ground`: TI calls it the common pin, normally connected to ground. The TL431 can also serve in negative references, which the type colour does not show. `CATHODE` and `REF` are `signal`.
- `COMMON` of the 78L05 follows TI's name; onsemi says Ground. The notes point out that the 78L05 runs opposite to the L78xx in TO-220, which follows from comparing the two items.
- LM35: `+VS` follows TI; the sheet's other packages (TO-CAN, SOIC, TO-220) have other numbers and are left out.
- Variant order follows the task.
