# ethernet_categories — Twisted-pair cable categories / Kategorien von Twisted-Pair-Kabeln

Kind: transcribed standard (TIA-568.2 / ISO/IEC 11801 categories and classes, IEEE 802.3 BASE-T reach) taken from official liaison and project documents plus maker and test-equipment documents
Guide-value note needed: yes for the construction column ("usual", not mandatory) and for the conditional reach cells (Cat 5e at 5 Gbit/s, Cat 6 at 10 Gbit/s)

## Purpose
One row per cable category: the frequency the category is specified to, the matching ISO/IEC 11801 class, the TIA status, the usual construction and connector, the specified channel length, and which BASE-T Ethernet PHYs run on it over which channel length. Answers "will 10GBASE-T run on my Cat 6?" and "what is Cat 7 in TIA terms?".

Key column: `category`. Bandwidth in Hz (100 MHz = 100000000), channel in m.
Reach cells are text on purpose: "100 m" = supported over that channel length; "–" = not specified for this category (checked); an empty cell (null) = UNVERIFIED. A reach cell with "(limited)" / "(eingeschränkt)" depends on alien crosstalk and must be tested in the field; the condition is in `note`.

- Bandwidth and class: every value has at least two independent documents — the official ISO liaison N638 (1995 and 2002 text: D 100, E 250, F 600 MHz) plus Fluke (D/E/EA/F/FA) and Panduit (E/EA/F/FA/Cat 8); 5e 100 MHz Fluke = Siemon; 7A 1000 MHz Panduit = Fluke = Siemon; Cat 8 / Class I and II 2000 MHz Panduit = CommScope = Fluke = Siemon.
- TIA status: Cat 7/7A ISO-only is stated by Panduit and Fluke (and Rybinski 2007: TIA not developing Category 7) — confirms the assumption. Cat 8.2 ISO-only follows from CommScope (TIA Category 8 = single RJ45 interface, Category 8.2 is an IEC component category for ISO Class II) and Fluke (TIA defines Category 8 channels, ISO the Class I/II channels). Cat 5 superseded: Fluke = OCC = Rybinski.
- Reach: 2.5/5GBASE-T IEEE objectives = Fluke blog = Cisco; 10GBASE-T TIA tutorial (in the IEEE 802.3an area) = Fluke = Cisco = Panduit; 25/40GBASE-T IEEE 802.3bq objectives = Panduit = CommScope = Fluke; 1000BASE-T on Cat 5 Fluke (quoting IEEE 802.3ab) = OCC. For categories above the one a PHY was written for, "100 m" rests on the ISO rule "links and channels of a given class will support all applications of a lower class" (ISO/IEC 11801:2002 §6.3, quoted in N638) together with Panduit Table 1 and, for Cat 8, CommScope.
- Disputed or conditional cells (all marked "(limited)" or explained in `note`):
  - Cat 6 / 10GBASE-T: IEEE/TIA design target "at least 55 m"; Fluke: about 37 m expected, 37–55 m depending on alien crosstalk; Panduit: "limited distances per TSB-155-A"; Cisco: 55 m. Shipped as "55 m (limited)".
  - Cat 5e / 5GBASE-T: IEEE objective "up to 100 m … on defined use cases"; Fluke the same; Cisco shows it without condition; CommScope rates Cat 5e as higher risk. Shipped as "100 m (limited)".
  - Cat 8 / 10GBASE-T and slower: the Category 8 channel is specified to 30 m (`channel` = 30), but Panduit and CommScope both state 100 m for 10GBASE-T and lower because Cat 8 parts also meet Category 6A. Shipped as 100 m with the explanation in `note`.
  - GG45: Panduit calls the Cat 7/7A connectors "Non-RJ45"; Fluke calls GG45 "an RJ45 style solution … backward compatible with existing RJ45 patch cords". Both agree that full Class F/FA performance needs the non-RJ45 contact set; the `interface` cell says "not RJ45: TERA … or GG45" and the note explains the RJ45 fallback.
  - Panduit's table labels the Cat 8 class "Class 8.I"; ISO/IEC 11801-1 (preview) and CommScope name it Class I. Class I used.

## Deliberate deviations
- Cat 8.1 and the TIA "Category 8" are one row (`cat8_1`): CommScope and Panduit show they correspond (ISO Class I, RJ45 IEC 60603-7-81). Cat 8.2 stays a separate row.
- Added columns `interface` and `channel`: without them Cat 7/7A/8.2 (non-RJ45) and Cat 8 (30 m channel) would be misleading.
- 2.5GBASE-T and 5GBASE-T get separate columns, because Cat 5e differs between them.
- PoE left out as a column: no read source gives PoE limits per category (Leviton gives 802.3af over Cat 3 or higher and 4-pair PoE generally; Panduit rates PoE only qualitatively). Kept as a note.
- No year column: makers disagree on introduction years (Siemon tech brief: 5e since 1999; Rybinski: 5e first published 2000); only uncontested years are in `tia`/`note` (Cat 6A 2008: Leviton = Siemon; Cat 8 2016: CommScope = Leviton).
- ISO class for Cat 5 is shown as "D (ISO/IEC 11801:1995)": since the 2002 edition the ISO Class D / "Category 5" equals TIA Cat 5e (N638 point 2).
- The research column `check` (cross-check per row) is not a column of the catalog table; its content is the Cross-check section above and the `ref` texts of the vectors. The catalog table also has no `id` column; the key column is `category`.
- Shipped as `guide: true`: the standards (IEEE 802.3, TIA-568.2, ISO/IEC 11801) were not read, the reaches quote task-force objectives and maker documents.

## UNVERIFIED (shipped as empty)
- `cat5.construction`: only the 1995 Spurgeon quick reference says Cat 5 is unshielded; no maker document for plain Cat 5 was found (current maker sheets are all Cat 5e).
- `cat8_2.e100`, `e1000`, `e2g5`, `e5g`, `e10g` (5 cells): Siemon says its Category 8.2 cords support 10BASE-T to 40GBASE-T and are backwards compatible with Category 7A/Class FA, but no second document states the reach of the lower-speed PHYs on Category 8.2 (inside the 30 m Class II channel or over a longer one).
- That makes 6 empty cells; additionally the exact shield type of Cat 8 (F/UTP vs S/FTP) is not shipped — `construction` says only "shielded" (Panduit "STP", Fluke "screened"; Siemon's 8.2 cable is S/FTP, single source).

## Notes (en / de)
- en: A category describes components (cable, connector), a class describes the installed channel or link. TIA uses categories for both; ISO/IEC 11801 uses classes for links and channels.
- de: Eine Kategorie beschreibt Komponenten (Kabel, Steckverbinder), eine Klasse die installierte Übertragungsstrecke bzw. den Kanal. Die TIA verwendet für beides Kategorien, ISO/IEC 11801 für Strecken und Kanäle Klassen.
- en: A channel is only as good as its weakest part: Cat 7/7A cable with RJ45 jacks or Cat 6A patch cords performs as Cat 6A. Higher classes support all applications of lower classes.
- de: Ein Kanal ist nur so gut wie sein schwächstes Teil: Cat-7/7A-Kabel mit RJ45-Buchsen oder Cat-6A-Patchkabeln verhält sich wie Cat 6A. Höhere Klassen unterstützen alle Anwendungen niedrigerer Klassen.
- en: Reach values are channel lengths. A 100 m channel is 90 m of solid installation cable, 10 m of cords and up to four connections (ISO/IEC 11801:2002 text quoted in WG3 N638); a Category 8 channel is 30 m with 2 connectors (24 m link + 6 m cords per Panduit COTB01).
- de: Die Reichweiten sind Kanallängen. Ein 100-m-Kanal besteht aus 90 m Installationskabel mit massiven Adern, 10 m Patch- und Geräteanschlusskabeln und bis zu vier Steckverbindungen (Text aus ISO/IEC 11801:2002, zitiert in WG3 N638); ein Kanal der Kategorie 8 hat 30 m mit 2 Steckverbindungen (laut Panduit COTB01 24 m Strecke + 6 m Patchkabel).
- en: PoE (IEEE 802.3af/at/bt) is not limited per category in the read sources; higher categories with larger conductors heat less in bundles, so Cat 6A or better is recommended for 4-pair PoE (Leviton, Siemon).
- de: PoE (IEEE 802.3af/at/bt) ist in den gelesenen Quellen nicht je Kategorie begrenzt; höhere Kategorien mit dickeren Adern erwärmen sich im Bündel weniger, daher wird für 4-Paar-PoE Cat 6A oder besser empfohlen (Leviton, Siemon).
- en: Construction codes as used by the makers read here: U/UTP has no shield (Prysmian UC300, Siemon Premium 5e); F/UTP has one overall foil around unshielded pairs (Siemon System 5e F/UTP, Rybinski 2007); S/FTP has an overall braid and a foil around each pair (Siemon TERA E10).
- de: Bauarten, wie sie die gelesenen Hersteller verwenden: U/UTP ist ungeschirmt (Prysmian UC300, Siemon Premium 5e); F/UTP hat eine Gesamtfolie um ungeschirmte Paare (Siemon System 5e F/UTP, Rybinski 2007); S/FTP hat ein Gesamtgeflecht und eine Folie um jedes Paar (Siemon TERA E10).
