## Cross-check
- Letters and names are definitional (source 1). English names are the standard English names.
- The `use` column is **typical** usage, assembled from sources 2–6; each listed use appears in at least one of them. Uses not found in those pages were not added (ι, ξ, ο, υ are marked "no common use"). Two entries are common textbook usage but **not** backed by a source I read: α "common-base current gain" and θ/ϑ "angle; temperature" — mark them UNVERIFIED or drop them (Θ "magnetomotive force / Durchflutung" is backed by source 3). Δ (difference) and Σ (sum) are general mathematical notation, also not tied to a source.
- German "Jota" is a common alternative spelling; source 1 itself uses only "Iota".

## Notes (en / de)
- en: Usage varies between fields and countries (e.g. conductivity is σ internationally, often γ or κ in German electrical engineering). Always check the symbol definitions of the document you are reading.
- de: Die Verwendung schwankt je nach Fachgebiet und Land (z. B. Leitfähigkeit international σ, in der deutschen Elektrotechnik oft γ oder κ). Maßgeblich sind die Formelzeichen-Definitionen des jeweiligen Dokuments.
- en: The micro sign µ (U+00B5) and the Greek small mu μ (U+03BC) look alike; the ohm sign Ω (U+2126) is canonically equivalent to Greek capital omega (U+03A9).
- de: Mikrozeichen µ (U+00B5) und griechisches μ (U+03BC) sehen gleich aus; das Ohm-Zeichen (U+2126) ist kanonisch gleich dem griechischen Omega (U+03A9).
