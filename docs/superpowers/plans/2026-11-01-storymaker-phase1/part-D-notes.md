## Style decisions

Flat vector explainer look, no outlines on silhouettes, a single second tone (`$outline` at 12 to 22 percent opacity), sparse white light tones, rounded forms, soft cheek blush. Facial line work is drawn as filled ribbons (`ribbon`), so every renderer gets round caps; only a few thin prop lines use strokes (they assume round caps and joins). Eyes: white sclera, big dark iris with a highlight, lids as skin-coloured caps. Palettes `sunny` (verbatim from §3b) and `calm`. ## What A and C must know about the format (additions to §3a; none changes it)

1. **Limb sides.** Views face right. `_r` is the character's right limb. In the **front** view `_r` sits on the screen **right** (x > 0, `_l` on the left); in `three_quarter` and `profile` `_r` is the **near** limb (drawn above the torso, on the back side at x < 0) and `_l` the far one. This keeps `_r` gestures (point, wave, present) visible in the default three-quarter view and pointing outward in the front view. Negative angles swing a limb towards +x (forward when facing right). Slot `z` is per view and encodes the near/far order.
2. **Slot params used.** `body hair eyes brows mouth hand top bottom shoes glasses facial_hair accessory`. No slot uses `hair_back` or `skin` as `param`: the back hair slot `hair_back` also has `param: "hair"`, so the key is the character's `hair` value (§3a is ambiguous for `hair_back`). Variants are keyed per slot name (`variants[slot][key][view]`), so the left and right hand/foot/limb slots have mirrored shapes.
3. **Eyes and brows keys** are `<character value>/<state>`: eyes `round|almond|big` × `neutral wide smile laugh sad angry lookup narrow worried`; brows `soft|bold|thin` × `neutral raised sad angry confused think worried determined`. Expression mouths beyond the 7 speech shapes: `smile laugh frown squiggle side wobble firm grit`. The expression table is in the catalog entries (`eyes`, `brows`, `mouth` names); `expr.happy` = smile / neutral / smile, `expr.surprised` = wide / raised / o as in §3b.
4. **End parts.** Hands hang at local `y = 135` of the lower arms, feet (shoes) at `y = 205` of the lower legs, for every body type; body types change rest offsets (`bodies[...].bones[...].rest`) and shapes keyed by `body`, never these two lengths. `bone.length` is informational.
5. **Seated poses.** `pose.sit` and `pose.typing` have `seated: true` and an extra field **`offset_y: 215`** (rig units, positive = down, same meaning as the cycle key `offset_y`: shifts the root); `rig.seat_drop` (215) in `rig/human.json` states the same number. Without it the figure floats 215 units above the floor. C must apply `pose.offset_y` (the office chair seat and desk top are drawn for it; `environments.test.mjs` pins the numbers: seat at 774 px, desk top at 698 px, desk spot (36, 84)).
6. **Pose names and facing.** Pose bones are written for the unmirrored right-facing frame. `point-right` raises the near arm forward; `point-left` raises the far-side arm (`upper_arm_l: 88`) and has `view: "front"`. When an actor faces left (mirror) the player should swap `point-left` and `point-right` so that the name keeps meaning the screen direction (recommended, not required). `view` on a pose (`front` for point-left, arms-crossed, hands-on-hips; `three_quarter` otherwise) is a hint for the best view; symmetric poses and `celebrate` only read well in the front view, asymmetric gestures in the three-quarter view.
7. **Shapes.** A shape draws its `fill` or `gradient` if present and its `stroke` if present; one with none is invalid (the validator rejects it). Strokes use round caps and joins. **Gradient stop colours may be `$tokens`** (environments use `[[0,'$bg'],[1,'$bg2']]`); C's `parts.js` must resolve tokens inside stops. Colours are `#rrggbb` or `#rrggbbaa` or `$token`.
8. **Environment tokens.** Shapes resolve from `env.tokens[variant]` then palette tokens. The day variant omits `bg`/`bg2` (the palette decides), the night variant overrides them. Every local token exists in both variants. Layers are listed in draw order (back to front) and `parallax` is per layer, not monotonic (`wall` 0.1, `floor` 1, `furniture` 0.7).
9. **Aspect overrides.** A 9:16 or 1:1 output shows a vertical slice of the 16:9 design frame as wide as the output is at the design height (31.6 and 56.3 percent of the width). `aspects[...].focus_x` is the slice centre; `spots` lists replacements for spots that would fall outside. Every spot (with its override) lies at least 3 percent inside the slice (tested in Go and Node). Only `9:16` and `1:1` exist (16:9 needs none).
10. **Catalog fields beyond §3.** Pose entries carry optional `view` and `offset_y` (and `seated` as in §3b). Text style `box` = `{fill, radius, pad}` with `radius` and `pad` as fractions of the stage height; `text.caption` uses `color: "#ffffff"` and `box.fill: "#1f2a44d9"`. The three text style usage examples: `title` is exactly the §3b example (no `style`), `caption` and `label` add `"style": "caption"` or `"label"` to the `show` beat, so **A must accept `style` (a text style name) on `show` beats with `text`**. Gesture and cycle keys end on the first key (`t = 1`); `cycle.walk` has `speed: 16` (percent of the design width per second at depth scale 1: stride 2·420·sin 18° = 260 units, 520 per cycle of two steps) and `offset_y: 21` at contact (positive = down, root drops while the legs are spread).
11. **Thumbnails** are plain SVG (`<svg xmlns viewBox width height>`, no script, no `<image>`, no `href`), deterministic, rendered by the same §3a code. Character thumbnails are cropped to head and torso.
12. **`child` body** exists in `params.body` and `bodies` but is a small adult-proportioned figure (scale 0.82, shorter thighs and upper arms); child-specific heads and garments belong to the catalog expansion. The skirt (`bottom: skirt`) is built for slim, average, elder and child bodies; a broad body would show trouser colour at the knee.
